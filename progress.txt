**Task:** Read all 730 lines of `backend/app/services/rule_engine.py`. Document every regex pattern and rule. Classify each as KEEP/REPLACE/REMOVE.
**File:** `backend/app/services/rule_engine.py` (730 lines)

### File Structure
- **Lines 1-26**: Imports + logger
- **Lines 29-33**: `RiskLevel` enum (RED/YELLOW/GREEN)
- **Lines 36-51**: `RulePattern` dataclass (v1 rule format)
- **Lines 53-68**: `SmartRule` dataclass (v2, extends RulePattern with negative_patterns, escalation/de-escalation)
- **Lines 71-94**: `RuleMatch` dataclass (output of matching, includes cache_key for AI responses)
- **Lines 97-113**: Risk escalation/de-escalation helpers (`_escalate_risk`, `_deescalate_risk`)
- **Lines 120-312**: `DEFAULT_RULES` list — 13 built-in rules (not 16 as PRD states)
- **Lines 315-730**: `RuleEngine` class

### All 13 DEFAULT_RULES — Regex Patterns Inventory

**1. `unlimited_liability` (RED, deal_breaker=True)**
- `\b(unlimited\s+liability)\b`
- `\b(no\s+(cap|limit|limitation)\s+(on|to)\s+liability)\b`
- `\b(without\s+limit(ation)?\s+of\s+liability)\b`
- `\b(liability\s+shall\s+not\s+be\s+limited)\b`
- **Classification: REPLACE-WITH-AI** — These catch obvious phrases but miss nuanced unlimited liability clauses. E.g., "The Vendor's aggregate liability hereunder shall not be subject to any monetary limitation" would NOT match any pattern. AI understands semantic equivalents.

**2. `unilateral_termination` (RED, deal_breaker=True)**
- `\b(terminate\s+(this\s+)?(agreement|contract)\s+(at\s+)?any\s+time)\b`
- `\b(termination\s+without\s+(cause|reason))\b`
- `\b(may\s+terminate\s+(immediately|forthwith)\s+without\s+(notice|cause))\b`
- **Classification: REPLACE-WITH-AI** — Misses "Either party reserves the right to discontinue this arrangement at its sole discretion" — semantically identical but uses different vocabulary. Also can't distinguish buyer vs seller termination rights (context-dependent risk).

**3. `broad_indemnification` (SmartRule, base YELLOW, can escalate to RED)**
- Positive patterns (4): `indemnify and hold harmless`, `defend, indemnify, hold harmless`, `indemnif(y|ication) against any and all`, `unlimited indemnif(y|ication)`
- Negative patterns (1): `mutual(ly)?.{0,80}limited\s+to` — suppresses if mutual+limited
- Escalation (3): `one-sided|unilateral`, `uncapped|without cap|no cap`, `any and all (loss|damage|claim|liabilit)`
- De-escalation (1): `mutual(ly)?.{0,120}capped? (at|to)`
- **Classification: REPLACE-WITH-AI** — This is the most sophisticated rule (SmartRule with escalation logic), but still can't handle: (a) clauses that describe indemnification without using the word "indemnify", (b) cross-reference indemnification ("as set forth in Section 8.2"), (c) carve-outs that change the risk profile.

**4. `ip_assignment` (RED, deal_breaker=True)**
- `\b(work(s)?\s+for\s+hire)\b`
- `\b(assign(s|ment)?\s+(of\s+)?all\s+(intellectual\s+property|IP))\b`
- `\b(all\s+(rights|IP|intellectual\s+property)\s+shall\s+(belong|vest)\s+to)\b`
- **Classification: REPLACE-WITH-AI** — Misses "any developments, inventions, or improvements made during the term shall become the exclusive property of Client" — no "work for hire" or "assign" keyword.

**5. `auto_renewal` (YELLOW)**
- `\b(auto(matic(ally)?)?[\s-]?renew(al|s|ed)?)\b`
- `\b(shall\s+(automatically\s+)?renew\s+for\s+(successive|additional))\b`
- `\b(renew(s|ed)?\s+automatically)\b`
- **Classification: KEEP** — Auto-renewal uses very standard language. Regex is fast and accurate here. False negatives unlikely.

**6. `assignment_restriction` (YELLOW)**
- `\b(may\s+not\s+assign)\b`
- `\b(no\s+assignment\s+without\s+consent)\b`
- `\b(assignment\s+(is\s+)?(prohibited|restricted))\b`
- **Classification: REPLACE-WITH-AI** — Needs context: "may not assign without prior written consent, such consent not to be unreasonably withheld" is standard and fine. "may not assign under any circumstances" is problematic. Regex can't distinguish.

**7. `non_compete` (YELLOW)**
- `\b(non[\s-]?compete)\b`
- `\b(shall\s+not\s+compete)\b`
- `\b(restriction\s+on\s+competition)\b`
- **Classification: REPLACE-WITH-AI** — Needs contextual analysis of scope, duration, geography. "Non-compete limited to 6 months in Mumbai" vs "Non-compete worldwide for 5 years" are drastically different risks.

**8. `exclusive_dealing` (YELLOW)**
- `\b(exclusive\s+(right|license|agreement))\b`
- `\b(sole\s+and\s+exclusive)\b`
- `\b(exclusivity\s+(period|term|clause))\b`
- **Classification: REPLACE-WITH-AI** — "Exclusive right" could be perfectly fine (exclusive license to use software) or dangerous (exclusive dealing arrangement). Context-dependent.

**9. `confidentiality_term` (YELLOW)**
- `\b(perpetual\s+confidentiality)\b`
- `\b(confidential(ity)?\s+(in\s+)?perpetuity)\b`
- `\b(survive\s+(indefinitely|forever|in\s+perpetuity))\b`
- **Classification: KEEP as pre-filter** — The keyword "perpetual" or "perpetuity" is a strong signal. But AI should evaluate whether perpetual confidentiality is actually problematic in context (trade secrets vs general business info).

**10. `non_solicitation` (YELLOW)**
- `\b(non[\s-]?solicit(ation)?)\b`
- `\b(shall\s+not\s+solicit)\b`
- `solicit.{0,100}(employee|personnel|staff|contractor)`
- `\b(solicit,?\s+(induce,?\s+)?(recruit|hire|employ))\b`
- `\b(recruit(ing)?\s+(any\s+)?employee)\b`
- **Classification: KEEP as pre-filter** — Keyword-based detection is appropriate. AI should evaluate scope/duration.

**11. `reverse_engineering` (YELLOW)**
- `\b(reverse\s+engineer(ing)?)\b`
- `\b(decompil(e|ation)|disassembl(e|y))\b`
- `\b(derive\s+source\s+code)\b`
- **Classification: KEEP** — Very specific technical terms. Regex is perfect here.

**12. `governing_law` (GREEN)**
- `\b(govern(ed|ing)\s+(by|law))\b`
- `\b(laws\s+of\s+(the\s+)?(state|country|jurisdiction))\b`
- `\b(applicable\s+law)\b`
- **Classification: KEEP** — Standard clause detection. GREEN risk = informational only. Regex appropriate.

**13. `notice_provision` (GREEN)**
- `\b(notice(s)?\s+(shall|must|should)\s+be\s+(sent|given|provided))\b`
- `\b(written\s+notice)\b`
- **Classification: KEEP** — Standard clause detection. GREEN risk = informational only.

### RuleEngine Class Methods Analysis

**`__init__` (lines 328-390)** — KEEP
- Loads rules (custom, expanded 75-rule library, or default 13)
- Compiles all regex patterns at init time (good for performance)
- Handles SmartRule negative/escalation patterns
- Robust error handling for invalid regex

**`evaluate` (lines 392-513)** — KEEP (but role should change)
- Normalizes text via `normalize_text()` before matching
- For each rule: finds all regex matches, applies negative suppression, expands to sentence, deduplicates
- SmartRule: checks escalation/de-escalation in context window
- **Key insight**: This should become a PRE-FILTER that feeds candidates to AI, not a standalone risk assessor. Currently its output goes directly to results.

**`_remove_overlapping_matches` (lines 515-547)** — KEEP
- Deduplicates within same rule using 100-char proximity threshold
- Good heuristic for sentence-level dedup

**`_dedupe_cross_rule_matches` (lines 549-566)** — KEEP
- Prevents same text being flagged by multiple rules
- Uses normalized text comparison

**`_expand_to_sentence` (lines 568-608)** — KEEP
- Expands regex match to surrounding sentence (max 300 chars)
- Sentence boundary detection via `.!?;`
- **Issue**: `;` as sentence boundary is debatable in legal text where semicolons separate list items within a single clause. Could cause truncated context.

**`get_risk_summary` (lines 610-622)** — KEEP (utility)

**`get_default_rules` (lines 624-627)** — KEEP (utility)

**`from_playbook_rules` (lines 629-730)** — KEEP (but needs migration path)
- Converts PlaybookRule DB objects into RulePattern/SmartRule instances
- Supports 3 match_type modes: `exact` (escapes regex chars), `fuzzy` (word boundary), `regex` (raw)
- Validates regex at compile time, skips invalid patterns
- **Critical finding**: This is where user-created playbook rules become regex matchers. If we migrate to AI-native playbooks, this method needs the biggest change.
- **Security note** (line 663-664): Raw regex patterns from users are compiled directly. No ReDoS protection. A malicious user could craft a catastrophic backtracking regex like `(a+)+$` that hangs the server.

### Summary Classification

| Classification | Count | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| **KEEP** | 5 | auto_renewal, reverse_engineering, governing_law, notice_provision, confidentiality_term (as pre-filter) |
| **REPLACE-WITH-AI** | 8 | unlimited_liability, unilateral_termination, broad_indemnification, ip_assignment, assignment_restriction, non_compete, exclusive_dealing, non_solicitation (keep as pre-filter) |
| **REMOVE** | 0 | None — all rules serve a purpose, but 8 should transition to AI-primary with regex as optional pre-filter |

### Critical Findings

1. **ReDoS vulnerability** (line 663-664): `from_playbook_rules()` compiles user-supplied regex patterns without ReDoS protection. A malicious regex could cause CPU exhaustion. This is a P0 security issue.

2. **Semicolon as sentence boundary** (line 588): Legal contracts use semicolons within clauses for list items. Using `;` as a sentence terminator truncates clause context, leading to incomplete match text being sent to AI for verification.

3. **13 rules, not 16**: The PRD mentions "16 built-in rules" but DEFAULT_RULES has only 13. The expanded library (rules_library.py, 75 rules) is the actual default.

4. **SmartRule escalation is order-dependent** (lines 463-490): De-escalation is checked FIRST and takes priority. If both escalation and de-escalation patterns match, de-escalation wins. This is intentional but not documented for users.

5. **from_playbook_rules uses rule.clause_type as the name** (line 705): `name=rule.clause_type` — this means the rule name shown to users is the clause_type, not the rule's actual title. Could cause confusing UI labels.

6. **Normalized text dedup is too aggressive** (lines 556-566): If two genuinely different clauses have the same normalized text (e.g., two "governing law" clauses in different sections), only the first is kept. This is a false-negative risk for contracts with repeated standard language.

## Iteration 17 — REGEX-3: clause_classifier.py Full Audit (968 lines)

**File:** `backend/app/services/clause_classifier.py` (968 lines)

### Architecture Overview
The clause classifier is a **separate system from the rule engine**. While rule_engine.py detects RISKS, clause_classifier.py classifies CLAUSE TYPES (is this an indemnification clause, a governing law clause, etc.). It's Stage 2 of the pipeline.

**Key design**: Two-tier pattern matching:
1. **Heading patterns** — match section headings (strong signal, 0.6 base score)
2. **Body patterns** — match clause body text (0.2 each, max 0.6)
3. **Weight multiplier** — per-rule priority (1.0-1.3)
4. **AI fallback** — clauses with confidence < 0.5 are flagged `needs_ai_review=True`

### Taxonomy: 30 Clause Types in 8 Groups + UNKNOWN
- Formation (5): definitions, recitals, entire_agreement, amendments, severability
- Term (6): duration, auto_renewal, termination_for_cause, termination_for_convenience, cure_period, survival
- Financial (5): payment_terms, late_payment, price_escalation, taxes, audit_rights
- Liability (4): liability_cap, consequential_damages, indemnification_scope, insurance
- IP (3): ip_ownership, license_grant, ip_indemnification
- Confidentiality (4): confidentiality_obligations, confidentiality_exceptions, data_protection, breach_notification
- Governance (4): governing_law, jurisdiction, arbitration, force_majeure
- Restrictive (3): non_compete, non_solicitation, exclusivity
- UNKNOWN (1): fallback

**Actual count: 34 types** (30 named + UNKNOWN), not exactly "30" as the docstring claims.
**Classifier rules: 30** `_ClassifierRule` instances (one per type except UNKNOWN).

### Classification: KEEP (with enhancements)
**This classifier should MOSTLY be KEPT.** Here's why:

1. **It classifies, not assesses risk.** "Is this an indemnification clause?" is a categorization task where keyword matching is fast and effective. This is fundamentally different from "Is this indemnification clause risky?"

2. **The AI fallback is well-designed.** Ambiguous clauses (confidence < 0.5) are already flagged for AI review. This is the right architecture.

3. **Section splitting is structural** — `_split_into_sections()` (lines 876-963) uses numbered headings, ALL CAPS, and article markers. This is the correct approach; AI would be overkill for section splitting.

### Critical Findings

**1. Scoring ceiling creates false ambiguity (lines 835-853)**
- Heading match: 0.6
- Body matches: 0.2 each, capped at 0.6
- Max possible: 0.6 + 0.6 = 1.2, but confidence capped at 1.0 (line 872)
- With weights (up to 1.3): max = 1.2 × 1.3 = 1.56, capped to 1.0
- **Issue**: A clause with NO heading (common in contracts) starts at 0.0 heading score. Maximum from body alone = 0.6 × weight. For weight=1.0, body-only max is 0.6 — meaning body-only classification is ALWAYS flagged as ambiguous if < 0.5 threshold needs 3 body matches. This means contracts with inline clauses (no headings) will flood the AI with "ambiguous" classifications, wasting tokens.

**2. `_split_into_sections` heading detection too rigid (lines 886-902)**
- Only detects: numbered (`1.`, `2.1`), ARTICLE/SECTION N, SCHEDULE A, Roman numerals, ALL CAPS
- Misses: Bold text (common in Word documents — but text extraction loses formatting), "Part X", "Appendix", "Addendum"
- **Indian contracts**: Often use "(a)", "(b)", "(i)", "(ii)" sub-numbering which won't be detected as headings
- ALL CAPS detection requires minimum 6 chars (line 929: `len(stripped) > 5`) — misses short headings like "SLA" or "TERM"

**3. Taxonomy gaps — 30 types vs 78 rules**
The classifier has 30 types, but the rules library has 78 rules. Missing clause types in classifier:
- No SaaS/Technology types (SLA, data portability, security standards, API rights)
- No compliance types (anti-bribery, sanctions, regulatory)
- No assignment/change of control types
- No transition assistance type
- No insolvency/bankruptcy type
- This means ~20+ rules from rules_library.py detect clauses that the classifier can't even categorize

**4. `UNKNOWN` maps to `ClauseGroup.FORMATION` (line 128)**
When a clause can't be classified, its group defaults to FORMATION. This is misleading — an unclassifiable clause about data breach notifications would appear under "Formation" in the UI.

**5. No de-duplication of section splits**
If a contract has "1. DEFINITIONS" followed by "1.1 Defined Terms", both are detected as separate sections with separate headings. The sub-section gets classified independently, potentially splitting a single logical clause into fragments.

**6. Singleton at module level (line 967)**
`clause_classifier = ClauseClassifier()` — compiled at import time. Good for performance (regex compiled once), but means rules can't be customized per-tenant without creating new instances.

### Pattern Quality Assessment
- **Heading patterns**: Generally well-crafted with appropriate `\b` boundaries
- **Body patterns**: More complex regex with lookaheads; good coverage of legal phrasing
- **Smart quote handling** (line 214): `["\u201c]\w+["\u201d]` — correctly handles Unicode smart quotes in definitions. Good.
- **Negative lookahead** (line 282): `\bterm\b(?!\s+(?:sheet|inati))` — prevents "termination" or "term sheet" from matching "Duration/term" heading. Smart.

### Verdict: KEEP but enhance
- **KEEP**: Entire classifier architecture, heading/body pattern approach, AI fallback
- **ENHANCE**: Add missing clause types (SaaS, compliance, assignment), fix UNKNOWN group mapping, improve section splitting for Indian contracts
- **REPLACE**: Nothing — clause classification is a categorization task well-suited to keyword matching

## Iteration 18 — REGEX-4: scope_analyzer.py Full Audit (451 lines)
**Task:** Read all 451 lines (not 406 as PRD states) of `backend/app/services/scope_analyzer.py`.
**File:** `backend/app/services/scope_analyzer.py` (451 lines)

### Architecture Overview
ScopeAnalyzer runs BETWEEN Stage 2 (rule engine) and Stage 3 (AI). It takes RuleMatch objects and analyzes each clause's **scope dimensions** using regex:
1. **Breadth** — narrow/standard/broad/unlimited
2. **Mutuality** — mutual/one-sided favorable/one-sided unfavorable/unclear
3. **Financial Exposure** — capped/uncapped/not applicable
4. **Duration** — fixed term/perpetual/survival/not specified
5. **Trigger** — material breach/any breach/negligence/gross negligence/willful misconduct

Each dimension produces a sub-score, and a weighted composite `scope_score` (1-10) determines `risk_adjustment` (-1, 0, or +1).

### Classification: REPLACE-WITH-AI (mostly)

**This is exactly the kind of analysis AI does better.** Scope analysis requires understanding SEMANTIC CONTEXT:

1. **Breadth detection fails on paraphrasing**: "any and all" is caught, but "the broadest extent permitted by law" is not. "Without limitation" is caught, but "there shall be no restriction on the types of claims covered" is not.

2. **Mutuality detection is too simplistic (lines 302-313)**:
   - Detects "each party" or "both parties" as mutual ✓
   - But MISSES asymmetric obligations phrased as separate sentences: "The Vendor shall indemnify the Client..." in one paragraph, with no corresponding "The Client shall indemnify the Vendor..." — this is one-sided, but the analyzer would mark it UNCLEAR since neither mutual nor one-sided patterns match.
   - **Critical flaw**: `ONE_SIDED_INDICATORS` (lines 166-171) hardcode party names: "company", "vendor", "provider", "licensor", "employer", "customer", "client", "licensee", "employee". What about "Seller", "Buyer", "Supplier", "Consultant", "Contractor", "Subscriber", "End User", "Recipient", "Disclosing Party"? These common terms are completely missed.

3. **Financial exposure cap extraction (lines 174-177)**:
   - Pattern captures dollar amounts and "months of fees" phrasing
   - **Good**: Handles multiple currencies (USD, INR, GBP, EUR, £, $, ₹)
   - **Good**: Handles Indian numbering (lakh, crore)
   - **Miss**: "aggregate liability shall not exceed the fees paid in the preceding twelve (12) month period" — the word "twelve" written out (not digit) won't match `\d+\s*months`
   - **Miss**: Percentage-based caps ("100% of fees paid under this SOW") won't match

4. **Duration detection has priority bug (lines 330-351)**:
   - Checks perpetual FIRST, survival SECOND, fixed-term THIRD
   - If a clause says "shall survive termination for a period of 3 years", it matches SURVIVAL (correct)
   - But if it says "this irrevocable license shall terminate after 5 years", "irrevocable" triggers PERPETUAL even though there's a fixed 5-year term
   - `irrevocable` ≠ `perpetual` in legal terms. An irrevocable license can still have a fixed term.

5. **Trigger detection conflates gross negligence and willful misconduct (line 198)**:
   `TriggerType.GROSS_NEGLIGENCE` patterns include `willful\s+misconduct` — this means gross negligence and willful misconduct are treated as equivalent triggers. But `TriggerType.WILLFUL_MISCONDUCT` also exists separately (line 199). Due to the check order in `_detect_trigger` (lines 356-361), WILLFUL_MISCONDUCT is checked first, so the duplicate in GROSS_NEGLIGENCE is dead code but confusing.

### Scoring Model Analysis (lines 363-404)

Weights: Breadth 25%, Mutuality 25%, Financial 25%, Duration 15%, Trigger 10%

**Risk adjustment thresholds**:
- scope_score >= 7.5 → escalate (+1)
- scope_score <= 3.0 → de-escalate (-1)
- Otherwise → no adjustment (0)

**Issue**: The 7.5 escalation threshold is quite high. Let's check worst case:
- Unlimited breadth (10.0 × 0.25 = 2.5)
- One-sided unfavorable (9.0 × 0.25 = 2.25)
- Uncapped exposure (9.0 × 0.25 = 2.25)
- Perpetual (9.0 × 0.15 = 1.35)
- Any breach (8.0 × 0.10 = 0.80)
- **Worst case: 9.15** → correctly escalates

Best case: Narrow(2×.25) + Mutual(3×.25) + Capped(3×.25) + Fixed(3×.15) + Willful(3×.10) = 0.5+0.75+0.75+0.45+0.3 = **2.75** → correctly de-escalates

**Middle ground concern**: A clause that is BROAD (7.5×.25=1.875) + ONE_SIDED_UNFAVORABLE (9×.25=2.25) + NOT_APPLICABLE financial (5×.25=1.25) + NOT_SPECIFIED duration (5×.15=0.75) + NONE_SPECIFIED trigger (5×.10=0.50) = **6.625** → NO adjustment. This seems too lenient — a broad, one-sided clause should probably escalate.

### Coverage Report (lines 406-446)
- Lists all 75 clause types and reports which are present/missing
- **Good**: Coverage note acknowledges not all types apply to every agreement
- **Issue**: The `ALL_CLAUSE_TYPES` list (lines 94-129) uses different IDs than the rules in rules_library.py. E.g., scope_analyzer uses "confidentiality" but rules_library uses "confidentiality_obligations". "assignment" vs "assignment_restriction". "sla" vs "sla_terms". This means coverage calculation may be inaccurate — types detected by rules won't match the strings in ALL_CLAUSE_TYPES.

### Critical Findings Summary

1. **Party name hardcoding** (lines 166-171): ONE_SIDED_INDICATORS only catch 5 party labels each for buyer/seller. Real contracts use dozens of different party designations. This causes most one-sided clauses to be classified as UNCLEAR.

2. **`irrevocable` ≠ `perpetual`** (line 183): False perpetual detection for irrevocable licenses with fixed terms.

3. **Clause type ID mismatch**: ALL_CLAUSE_TYPES uses different IDs than rules_library.py, causing inaccurate coverage metrics.

4. **Written-out numbers not captured**: "twelve months" won't match the `\d+\s*months` pattern.

5. **Context window is fixed at 500 chars** (lines 234-236): For long clauses, 500 chars before/after may not capture the full scope. An indemnification clause spanning 2000 chars would have scope analyzed based on only the edges.

### Verdict
- **REPLACE-WITH-AI**: Breadth, mutuality, and financial exposure analysis — these require semantic understanding
- **KEEP as pre-filter**: Duration detection (structural patterns), trigger detection (specific legal terms), coverage report (taxonomy check)

## Iteration 19 — REGEX-5: defined_terms_resolver.py Full Audit (456 lines)
**Task:** Read all 456 lines of `backend/app/services/defined_terms_resolver.py`. Document defined term extraction.
**File:** `backend/app/services/defined_terms_resolver.py` (456 lines, not 434 as PRD states)

### Architecture Overview
4-step pipeline:
1. **Extract** — 7 regex patterns match "Term" means/shall mean/is defined as/refers to/hereinafter/colon-separated definitions
2. **Cross-reference** — Finds which definitions reference other defined terms
3. **Resolve** — Recursively expands cross-references up to depth 3
4. **Flag overbroad** — 9 regex patterns detect dangerously broad definition language

Output feeds into AI prompts via `to_prompt_context()` — this gives the AI visibility into what "Confidential Information" or "Intellectual Property" ACTUALLY means in this specific contract.

### Classification: KEEP

**This is an ideal regex use case.** Defined terms follow highly predictable syntactic patterns:
- `"Capitalized Term" means ...`
- `"Capitalized Term" shall mean ...`
- `(hereinafter "Term")`

Regex is FASTER, MORE RELIABLE, and CHEAPER than AI for this extraction. The output feeds into AI prompts, which is the correct architecture.

### Detailed Pattern Analysis

**7 Definition Extraction Patterns (lines 108-158):**

1. `"Term" means/shall mean/will mean ...` — Core pattern, handles most definitions ✓
2. `"Term" is/are defined as ...` — Alternative definition syntax ✓
3. `As used herein, "Term" means/refers to ...` — Inline definition ✓
4. `"Term" has/shall have the meaning ...` — Cross-reference style ✓
5. `"Term" refers to ...` — Simple reference ✓
6. `(hereinafter "Term")` — Parenthetical definition ✓ — **Note**: Groups are REVERSED (definition first, term second). Code handles this correctly (lines 269-274).
7. `"Term": definition` — Colon/em-dash separated ✓

**All patterns handle smart quotes** (`\u201c`, `\u201d`) ✓

**Definition boundary detection**: Uses lookahead `(?=\n["\u201c][A-Z]|\n\n|\n\d+\.\s|\Z)` — stops at:
- Next quoted capitalized term on new line
- Double newline (paragraph break)
- Numbered section heading
- End of text

### Critical Findings

**1. Definition boundary fails for inline definitions (all patterns)**
The lookahead `\n\n|\n\d+\.\s` assumes definitions are paragraph-separated. But many contracts have INLINE definitions:
> `"Confidential Information" means all information disclosed by either party, whether written or oral, and "Receiving Party" means the party receiving such information.`
In this case, the regex would capture EVERYTHING after "Confidential Information means" all the way to the end of the paragraph, including the definition of "Receiving Party". The second term's definition would then be a subset of the first.

**2. Term name length limit of 80 chars (line 111)**
Pattern: `[A-Z][A-Za-z\s&,/()-]{1,80}?`. Indian contracts sometimes have long defined terms:
- `"Minimum Guaranteed Annual Revenue Commitment"` (46 chars) ✓
- `"Intellectual Property Rights and Proprietary Information of the Disclosing Party"` (82 chars) ✗ — would be truncated

**3. Parenthetical pattern (Pattern 6) captures too much context (line 147)**
Pattern: `([A-Za-z][^.]{10,200}?)` captures 10-200 chars BEFORE the parenthetical as the "definition". This could match half a sentence that isn't really a definition:
> `The parties agree that all software code developed during the engagement (hereinafter "Deliverables") shall be...`
Captured "definition" would be: `The parties agree that all software code developed during the engagement` — this is context, not a precise definition.

**4. Colon pattern (Pattern 7) has no uppercase restriction on right side (line 154)**
Could match section headings like `"DEFINITIONS": This section defines the terms...` where the text after the colon is not actually a definition of the term "DEFINITIONS".

**5. Overbroad detection is well-calibrated (lines 161-198)**
9 signals with a 2-signal threshold for flagging (or 1 high-severity signal). Covers:
- "all information", "any and all", "without limitation"
- "whether or not marked", "including but not limited to"
- "in any form or medium", "directly or indirectly"
- "all intellectual property", "everything/totality"
**Assessment**: These are exactly the right phrases that a corporate lawyer would flag. Well-done.

**6. Recursive resolution has circular reference protection (line 406)**
`visited` set prevents infinite loops. MAX_RECURSION_DEPTH=3 prevents runaway expansion. Both are correct safeguards.

**7. `to_prompt_context()` truncates definitions at 400 chars (line 63)**
For a contract with 50 defined terms averaging 200 chars each, the prompt context would be ~10,000 chars (~2,500 tokens). This is a meaningful chunk of the AI's context window but within budget.

**8. Missing pattern: Bold/underlined defined terms**
Many Word documents use **bold** or __underline__ to indicate defined terms without quotes. Since text extraction strips formatting, these terms would be invisible to the resolver. This is a limitation of the text extraction layer, not the resolver itself.

### Verdict: KEEP
- **KEEP**: All 7 extraction patterns, cross-reference resolution, overbroad detection
- **ENHANCE**: Fix inline definition boundary, increase term name length limit
- **REPLACE**: Nothing — defined term extraction is a textbook regex use case
- **Integration with AI is correct**: Resolver output feeds into AI prompts via `to_prompt_context()`, giving AI the definitions it needs for contextual analysis
